Brian Deuksin Kwon

Ph.D. Candidate in Computer Science · University of Southern California · USC ICT

I am a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at the University of Southern California, advised by Prof. Gale M. Lucas at the USC Institute for Creative Technologies, and I work closely with Prof. Jonathan Gratch and Prof. Mohammad Soleymani. I expect to graduate in 2027.

My research aims to build socially intelligent AI agents that understand, simulate, and shape human decision-making and social influence — in negotiation, persuasion, and collaboration — while staying grounded in how real people think and behave. My work spans:

  • AI Agents — multi-agent systems, strategic reasoning, tool-augmented agents, policy learning, and LLM post-training.
  • Human-Centered AI — human behavior simulation, persona- and personality-conditioned agents, and human-referenced evaluation of behavioral fidelity.
  • Multimodal AI — multimodal interaction modeling and affective computing.

I am a founding organizer of the Workshop on Social Influence in Conversations (SICon), which I have co-organized for all three editions to date — ACL 2023, EMNLP 2024, and ACL 2025 (Program Chair, 2025).

Before my Ph.D., I spent four years as an ML/NLP Research Engineer on SK Telecom’s Foundation Model team, where I pretrained foundation LLMs from scratch and built RAG-based long-term-memory conversational agents for the publicly launched AI assistant A. I also did doctoral studies at the University of Chicago. Most recently, I was an AI Research Intern at Honda Research Institute (Summer 2026), working on closed-loop multi-agent human behavior simulation and human-aligned LLM post-training.

Selected Publications

  1. EMNLP
    ARIA: Aligning Emotional Support Policies with Implicit Multimodal Affective Recovery
    Deuksin Kwon, K. Joo, E. H. Lee, Mohammad Soleymani, and Gale Lucas
    In Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: EMNLP 2026, 2026
    TL;DR Link

    A framework that models implicit user preferences from multimodal behavioral feedback (facial expressions, vocal cues), using cross-modal consensus and uncertainty-aware filtering to construct reliable preference pairs for DPO-based LLM policy optimization.

    ASTRA: A Negotiation Agent with Adaptive and Strategic Reasoning via Tool-integrated Action for Dynamic Offer Optimization
    Deuksin Kwon, J. Hae, E. Clift, D. Shamsoddini, Jonathan Gratch, and Gale Lucas
    In Proceedings of the 2025 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing, 2025
    TL;DR Link

    A stateful, tool-augmented LLM agent with online belief-state updates (partner preferences, fairness, stance), solver-backed tool use, and look-ahead simulation for strategic action selection. Improves agent utility from 20.75 to 24.31 (+17.2%) in agreement cases.
